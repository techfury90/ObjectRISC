# Welcome to Your Object RISC Workstation

Welcome. You are now the owner of a serious computer. Your Object RISC workstation places the full power of a dedicated processor on your desk — yours alone, answering to no one else, waiting on no distant mainframe for its turn. Unlike the timesharing systems of the past, where you contended with strangers for a sliver of someone else's machine, the computer before you is undivided. Its memory, its display, and its every cycle are at your command. We believe you will find this a profound change. We built it to be one.

## What You Have

At the heart of your workstation is Object RISC, a processor of the new reduced-instruction-set tradition — lean, fast, and built for the work ahead. But Object RISC carries a commitment its contemporaries do not. In this machine, every resource — a file, a window, a running program, a region of memory — is named by an object reference: an unforgeable token that confers exactly the authority it carries, and not one bit more. A reference cannot be guessed, forged, or counterfeited from an ordinary number. Programs hold only the references they were given, and can reach only what those references permit. This is capability security, and it is woven into the silicon itself, not bolted on afterward. The result is a system in which trust is precise and authority does not leak.

Your workstation is brought to life by Ouroboros, its operating system, raised upward from the bare processor — privilege modes, tasks, and services, every layer honoring the same capability discipline as the hardware beneath it.

## Your Desktop

Before you is an OPEN LOOK desktop, in the graphical tradition advanced by Sun, AT&T, and Xerox — the workstation revolution's own visual language. Your programs run in overlapping windows, each framed by a beveled title bar; you may raise, move, and arrange them as the work demands.

Press and hold the right mouse button anywhere on the desktop background to raise the Workspace menu — your starting point for the system's facilities.

Where a window holds more than it can show, an OPEN LOOK scrollbar appears at its side. Drag the elevator to move freely through the contents; click a line arrow to step gently, or hold it to scroll continuously; click the cable above or below the elevator to advance a full page; and click an end anchor to leap directly to the top or the bottom.

You already command a shell for issuing commands, a text editor for your work, and the Markdown viewer now presenting these very words.

## Going Forward

Take your time. Explore the desktop, raise the Workspace menu, and make the machine your own. You stand at the beginning of the workstation era, and this computer was built to meet it. We are proud to place it in your hands.
